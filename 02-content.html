What is Payday Super?

Payday Super is a reform to how superannuation guarantee (SG) contributions are made by employers in Australia. Under the new legislation, employers will be required to pay super guarantee contributions on the same day as ordinary salary and wages, rather than on a quarterly basis.

Why it matters

This change represents one of the most significant shifts in employer obligations since the introduction of compulsory superannuation in 1992. It aims to improve retirement outcomes for employees by ensuring super is paid more frequently and with greater transparency. For more context, read our in-depth blog article on Payday Super.

Key Dates and Timeline

The Payday Super legislation was introduced to the Australian Parliament in late 2025. Here are the key dates your payroll team needs to know:

Critical milestones

  • 1 July 2026: Payday Super legislation takes effect
  • 1 January 2026: Recommended start date for payroll system readiness testing
  • Q1 2026: Final ATO guidance expected on compliance requirements
  • Ongoing: Employers must ensure SG payments align with each pay cycle

Compliance Obligations

The seven-day payment window

Under Payday Super, employers must ensure that superannuation guarantee contributions are made within seven calendar days of paying an employee their salary or wages. This replaces the current quarterly deadline of 28 days after the end of each quarter.
